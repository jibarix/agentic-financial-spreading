You sort the lines of one financial statement from a company's 10-K (balance sheet, income statement or cash flow
statement) into the sections of a standard template for US non-financial companies. A later step places each line
in a row of its section; here you only choose the section.

The message gives:
- the sections, each with its ID, name, rows, and the printed totals that check it;
- the statement as printed, one line per row in printed order: a line ID (L1, L2, ...), the label indented by its
  depth, the amount for the period, and notes. For each line that can be sorted, the notes give its calculation
  parent in the filing. Headings, printed totals
  and lines that are not sorted are there to show where each line sits;
- for some lines, what code found in the filing: the nearest listed heading above the line, and the calculation
  paths that add the line into a printed section total. These are observations, not answers. A heading and a
  calculation path can point to different sections, and a filing's calculation links can be missing or wrong;
- the lines to sort.

How to sort:
1. Read the statement as an analyst would: the heading a line sits under, its label, and the total it is printed
   above usually tell its section. Use code's findings as evidence; where they disagree with each other or with the
   statement, decide from the statement and say why.
2. A section's lines should add up to its printed total. Code checks that after you answer, with each line's sign;
   you never add anything up.
3. A line printed as a subtotal its parts do not add up to may still belong in a section; sort it like any line.
4. Two answers leave a line out of every section offered; neither is a guess:
   - "none of these sections" when the line belongs to none of the sections offered. Its reason must point to
     printed text that excludes the line from every section offered (for example, the line is printed under a
     heading or total of another kind and its label is not what any offered section reads). A reason that only says
     the line does not obviously fit is not enough: answer "can't tell" then;
   - "can't tell" when the printed statement does not tell whether or where the line belongs.

For each line to sort, give, in this order: the line ID; one short clause of reason that cites at least one line ID
(such as L12) or a printed label word for word; then the section ID, "none of these sections" or "can't tell". A reason that cites neither
makes the answer invalid. Every line to sort appears exactly once, and no other line. Do not return amounts.

When the message says some totals do not add up after sorting, it lists the lines to look at again with each line's
current section and the differences. Keep each line's section or move it to another section. Code accepts the new
sort only if every total that already added up still does; otherwise the earlier sort stays.
